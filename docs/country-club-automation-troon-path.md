# What to sell, and how to reach Troon

**Date:** 10 September 2026
**Follows on from:** `country-club-automation-research.md`
**Question answered:** what is the single product to sell, does Troon's resort and hotel side widen the opportunity, and what is a realistic route in.

---

## 1. Sell one thing: fast answers to event and wedding enquiries

Everything else in the original concept note is a second-year upsell. The product that survives contact with the market is answering event enquiries in minutes instead of days.

**Why this one and not the phone concierge:**

| Reason | Detail |
|---|---|
| No direct competitor in this niche | The four AI voice competitors (GOLF.AI, AceCall, Tee Time AI, NeverClosed) are all pro shop and tee sheet focused. None lead with event enquiry response |
| No price anchor to lose against | Nobody is selling event response at $99/month. The phone concierge has that anchor and cannot escape it |
| Needs no integration | Web form, email, SMS, phone. Never touches their booking or accounting system |
| It is measurable | Time to first reply, and bookings won, are both countable before and after. That is a slide with a number on it |
| It works remotely | Asynchronous, written, provable on public information. It does not require being in the room in Arizona |

**The evidence:**

| Metric | Figure | Source context |
|---|---|---|
| Hotel and resort RFPs that go completely unanswered | 55% | Not declined, simply ignored |
| RFPs awarded to one of the first three responders | 61% | Cvent data |
| Competitive RFPs won by the first responder | 72% | HippoRev analysis |
| Median first reply, wedding venues | 11 hours | 1,200+ venue benchmark |
| Wedding venues replying within one hour | 37% | Same benchmark |
| Typical reply time | 42 hours | Same benchmark |
| Revenue per lost booking | $8,000–20,000 | Per wedding or private event |
| Planners citing slow response as a frustration | 24% | Cvent 2025 planner survey |
| Planner expectation for an RFP answer | within 4 business days | Cvent |

Sources: [Amadeus — win more RFP bids](https://www.amadeus-hospitality.com/insight/win-more-rfp-bids/), [Cvent — RFP response automation](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/hospitality/rfp-response-automation), [HippoRev — the 4-day delay that kills hotel sales](https://hipporev.ai/blog/4-stage-leak-analysis-hotel-rfp-response), [Wedding venue response benchmark](https://everybooking.com/blog/wedding-venue-inquiry-response-time-benchmark)

## 2. Troon is far larger than the concept note assumed

The original note treated this as a private club play. Troon is not primarily a private club company.

| Fact | Figure |
|---|---|
| Locations managed | 950+ |
| US states | 45+ |
| Countries | 40+ |
| 18-hole equivalent courses | 575+ |
| Food and beverage operations | 600+ |
| Headquarters | Scottsdale, Arizona |

Beyond golf, Troon runs homeowner association management, private residence clubs, estate management and associated hospitality venues. Brands include Troon Golf, Troon Privé, Troon International, Indigo Sports, CADDIEMASTER, ClubUp, Cliff Drysdale Tennis, Peter Burwash International, True Club Solutions, RealFood Hospitality Strategy and Design, Casa Verde Golf, ICON Management and Eventive Sports.

**Why the resort side matters more than the club side:** resorts host far more outside weddings and corporate events than member-governed private clubs do, and they want that business rather than restricting it. The 600+ food and beverage operations figure is the relevant number, not the course count. Every one of those is a venue that takes event enquiries.

## 3. The competition on the resort side is real, and there is still a gap

Do not repeat the mistake of the original note. Check before committing. The hotel group sales space is more mature than the golf space:

| Competitor | What they do |
|---|---|
| HippoRev | AI sales agent for hotel group business. Claims RFP processing cut from ~37 minutes to ~4 minutes, proposals with PMS pricing and personalised video |
| Amadeus Delphi | The incumbent in brand-mandated hotel environments |
| Thynk | For groups standardising on Salesforce |
| Cvent | Owns the RFP distribution channel itself, plus response tooling |

**The gap they leave open, and it is a real one:**

- These tools serve properties with a **dedicated group sales desk and a connected property management system**. A golf club banquet operation has neither.
- They are built for the **corporate RFP channel**, principally enquiries arriving through Cvent. The wedding enquiry that lands in a golf club's website contact form at 9pm on a Sunday is outside that pipeline entirely.
- They are **hotel-shaped**. Clubhouse banquet space, member-sponsored events, golf outings and tournament packages are not what Delphi was designed around.

So the honest positioning is narrow and defensible: **social and banquet event enquiries arriving by web form, email and phone at golf and club venues that have no group sales desk.** Not hotel rooms. Not corporate RFPs through Cvent. That is a real, unserved slice, and it is exactly what a 600-venue food and beverage portfolio is full of.

Sources: [HippoRev](https://hipporev.ai/), [HippoRev — AI hotel RFP tools for multi-property teams](https://hipporev.ai/blog/ai-hotel-rfp-tools-multi-property), [Cvent supplier and venue solutions](https://www.cvent.com/en/supplier-venue)

## 4. How to actually reach Troon

Cold-approaching head office as an unknown solo vendor with no reference customer does not work. The route is three steps, in order.

### Step one: land two or three individual Troon-managed properties

Not Troon corporate. Individual resorts and clubs on their roster.

- Property level general managers and directors of sales and catering can usually approve a small monthly operating expense without corporate procurement.
- Pick properties that publicly market weddings, have a web enquiry form, and are slow to answer it.
- Sell it as a paid pilot at a low price. The goal is a number, not margin.
- Instrument everything from day one: time to first reply before, time after, enquiries handled, bookings attributed.

This is the only step that matters. Without a Troon-managed property already using it, steps two and three are cold calls.

### Step two: go up with the number

Once two or three properties have results, approach corporate with a one-page case study naming the properties.

Publicly listed contact routes:

- **Troon Partners Network business development, US:** 480.477.0505. Note this network appears oriented toward course owners seeking management services, so treat it as a switchboard into business development rather than a vendor programme.
- **Troon Supplier Diversification Initiative** exists on their site and is the nearest thing to a published supplier door.
- **Named technology leadership:** Chris Nielsen (Chief Information Officer), Bryan Rollins (VP Information Technology), Timothy Weible (VP Enterprise Technology and Security), Jocelyn Guzman (VP Data Analytics and App Dev). Tim Schantz is CEO.

**Caveat on those names:** they come from third-party directory sites (TheOrg, ZoomInfo, RocketReach), not from Troon. Verify every one on LinkedIn before using it. People move.

The pitch at this level is not the product. It is **portfolio consistency**: one enquiry-response standard across 950 locations, reportable. That is an operator-level metric a single property cannot buy and a competitor selling per-property software does not offer.

### Step three: be where they are

Four events, in calendar order. These are where club and golf operators actually evaluate technology.

| Event | When and where | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| PGA Show | January, Orlando | 1,000+ exhibitors, broadest golf industry buyer audience |
| Golf Business Conference | January, Orlando | Owner and operator focused |
| CMAA World Conference and Club Business Expo | 2026 edition in Anaheim | The club management buyer, named in the original concept note |
| Golf Business Technology Conference | 2026 | B2B, technology-specific, smallest and most targeted |
| HITEC | 15–18 June 2026, San Antonio | Hospitality technology. Relevant if the resort side becomes the focus |

For a first year on a thin budget, attend rather than exhibit. The Golf Business Technology Conference is the cheapest place to meet the right buyer.

## 5. Honest read

The Troon route is correct in principle and slow in practice. A 950-location operator does not sign an unproven solo vendor, and the first property pilot is the entire bottleneck. Everything above is downstream of getting two or three paying properties that will let their names be used.

If that first pilot cannot be landed in about eight weeks, the finding is not that the route is wrong. The finding is that this vertical needs a US-based partner with existing relationships, and that is a different decision than the one the original concept note was asking about.
