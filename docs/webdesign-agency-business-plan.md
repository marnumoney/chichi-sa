## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The business:** A niche-focused, AI-run web design subscription targeting **home service contractors** (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical) as the primary vertical, with **dental and small healthcare practices** as a secondary vertical — both in the US/UK/AU/CA markets. Not a generalist "web design agency" — generalist positioning is hyper-competitive and undifferentiated worldwide. A named niche with proven budgets and proven bad-website pain is not.

**The model:** Flat-fee monthly subscription — $497 (Starter) to $997 (Standard) to $2,497 (Pro) — sold as "your website, built fast and cared for forever," not as a cheap commodity build. International USD revenue is the entire business; South Africa shrinks to 2-3 founding case-study clients only, used for testimonials and pipeline-stress-testing, not counted in growth projections.

**The unfair advantage:** `~/webdesign-ai` is a live, production-tested Claude-powered autonomous agent already managing a real site in production. It detects errors, rage-clicks, and performance issues and patches them without human intervention. No competitor in the productized-subscription space (DesignJoy, Penji, Design Pickle) offers self-healing sites. Combined with a South African cost base against US-dollar pricing, this yields **~88-90% gross margins** while pricing **roughly 50% below** a typical US small-agency website retainer ($1,000-2,000/mo) and 5-8x below DesignJoy-tier pricing ($4,995-7,995/mo) for a comparable "always-on" promise.

**Year 1, realistic case, modeled honestly at 10% monthly churn** (not the softer 5% assumption a first-draft plan would lean on): **~27 active clients, ~$20,300 MRR by month 12**, on a blended ARPU of ~$750/client. Conservative case: ~17 clients, ~$13,050 MRR. Optimistic case: ~37 clients, ~$27,983 MRR. Fixed costs are ~$200-300/month — this is net-positive from client one in every scenario.

**The 90-day sprint:** 2-3 SA founding clients for case studies (weeks 1-2), niche-specific cold outreach + Fiverr/Upwork presence live from week 1 (not month 6), Paddle merchant-of-record live for global billing by week 3, 8-10 paying international clients by day 90.

### 1.2 The AI Builder Landscape (Why It Doesn't Kill You)

The market has split into three lanes: design-first visual builders (Framer, Webflow), all-in-one convenience builders (Wix ADI, Hostinger, Durable, 10Web), and code-first full-stack generators (v0, Lovable, Bolt.new, Replit). Busy contractors and dentists do not have that time or that taste. **The managed-service layer on top of these tools is what you are selling — you are not competing with the builders, you are the reason a time-poor niche SMB never has to touch one.**

### 1.3 Why These Two Verticals

**Primary: Home service contractors (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, landscaping).**
- Most home service businesses spend **$2,500-$10,000/month** on marketing agency retainers; 8-15% of gross revenue on marketing overall, rising to 15-20% for businesses under two years old. 72% plan to *increase* marketing budgets in 2026.
- A contractor spending $8,000/month on marketing already allocates roughly **$1,600-2,400/month** to website-related work — our $497-997 tier is a fraction of a budget line they already approve, not a new expense category to sell them on.
- These are urgent, mobile-first, local searches ("plumber near me, emergency") — a broken or slow site directly costs them jobs, which makes the pitch concrete and provable rather than abstract.
- Page structure is highly templatable (services, service area, reviews, quote form) — ideal for an AI-driven build pipeline; low creative-judgment variance per site.
  Sources: [Hook Agency — Digital Marketing Costs for Home Service Businesses 2026](https://hookagency.com/blog/digital-marketing-costs-for-home-service-business/), [WebFX — 2026 Marketing Budget & Spend Insights for Home Services](https://www.webfx.com/blog/home-services/marketing-budget-handbook/), [WebFX — 2026 Home Services Marketing Benchmarks](https://www.webfx.com/blog/home-services/home-services-marketing-benchmarks/)

**Secondary: Dental and small healthcare practices.**
- Nearly 77% of patients research a dentist's website online before calling — a bad or absent site is a direct, provable revenue leak, same sales logic as contractors.
- Healthcare-adjacent digital spend is growing fast (28% projected YoY in 2026 for the broader healthcare vertical-software space), and practices already pay recurring fees for practice-management and patient-communication tools ($79-199/mo seat pricing, $149-549/mo add-ons) — they are pre-sold on the idea of paying monthly for digital infrastructure, which lowers the objection to a monthly website+care subscription specifically.
- Similarly templatable page structure (services, staff bios, booking CTA) with a stronger trust/credibility design requirement than contractors — good second niche to cross-sell design-quality positioning once case studies exist.
  Source: [worldmetrics.org — Best Dental Website Build Services 2026](https://worldmetrics.org/service/dental-website-build/)

**Why only these two, not "web design agency" broadly:** a generalist positioning competes with every AI builder and every existing agency on price and breadth simultaneously — a losing fight for a solo operator. A named vertical lets outreach copy, templates, case studies, and even the sales pitch itself compound instead of resetting for every new industry.

## 3. SERVICE OFFERING & PRICING

### 3.1 One Front Door: Subscription Only

**Critical fix from the prior draft:** the earlier version offered a $497/month "Starter" subscription *alongside* a $515 one-off build — those two offers directly cannibalize each other; no rational buyer picks the recurring option when a near-identical one-time price exists. **This revision removes the cheap one-off entirely.** There is exactly one front door: the subscription. A one-time buyout exists only as an *exit* option, priced deliberately above several months of subscription so it never undercuts the recurring offer (see 3.3).

### 3.2 Subscription Tiers (USD, the entire business)

| Tier | Price/month | What it actually includes (ongoing value, not just "the build") |
|------|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Starter** | $497/mo | Launch build (5-7 days) + 1 active request in queue at a time + 5-7 day turnaround on any future request + autonomous monitoring (webdesign-ai watches 24/7, auto-fixes Level-1 issues — see §4.5) + monthly uptime/traffic report |
| **Standard** | $997/mo | Everything in Starter + 48-72h turnaround + unlimited brands/pages + priority queue + local SEO basics (Google Business Profile optimization) + quarterly strategy check-in |
| **Pro** | $2,497/mo | Everything in Standard + 2 concurrent requests + same-day small fixes + custom booking/quote-form integrations + dedicated Slack/Discord channel |

**Why this resolves the contradiction:** every tier is framed around what continues every month (turnaround speed, monitoring, care, reporting) rather than around "a website" as a one-time deliverable. The build is the entry event; the subscription is the product. A client who cancels after month 1 got a site built — but loses monitoring, the fix queue, and the report the moment they leave, which is the actual retention lever.

### 3.3 Exit / Buyout Option (not a front door)

If a subscriber wants to leave and keep the site with no further relationship: **$2,500 one-time buyout** (site files + a 30-day handoff window, no further care). Priced at roughly 2.5-5 months of subscription specifically so staying is always the obviously better deal — this number exists to protect the subscription, not to compete with it.

### 3.4 South Africa Lane — Case Studies Only, Not a Growth Channel

**2-3 founding clients only**, ZAR-priced (R6,999-R9,999/mo, roughly the USD Standard/Pro tiers at local purchasing power), recruited from Sir's existing network in weeks 1-2. Purpose: stress-test the full build+care pipeline end-to-end fast, and generate the first 2-3 case studies/testimonials needed for outreach copy before the first international client closes. **Not included in any Year-1 growth projection in §8** — treat as R&D/pipeline-validation cost, not revenue strategy.

## 4. THE AUTOMATED DELIVERY PIPELINE

### 4.1 What You Already Own

The `~/webdesign-ai` project is a live, production-tested Claude-powered autonomous agent:

- **`agent/agent.js`** — Express server wrapping the Anthropic SDK. Monitors registered client sites 24/7. Detects JS errors, rage-clicks, user-stuck events (45s+ idle), performance issues (LCP > 2.5s). On any trigger: reads site files, has Claude diagnose root cause, writes and applies a targeted patch, validates, iterates until passing, notifies the owner only after verified.
- **`tracker/tracker.js`** — client-side tracking snippet injected into every managed site, streams behavior events to the agent. **Must be consent-gated for EU/UK visitors — see §7.4.**
- **`chat-widget/chat-widget.js`** — embeddable support chat widget for client sites.
- **`sites.config.js`** — one-line entry per client (`siteId`, `filesPath`, `ownerEmail`) → monitored immediately on next agent restart.
- **Currently managing one real site in production** (a South African client, PayFast checkout integration) — this is proven, not theoretical.

**Hardening needed before scaling to an international, multi-client roster:**
- Replace flat `sites.json`/`logs.json` files with a real per-client data model (SQLite or Supabase free tier) — mandatory before client #4-5 regardless of geography.
- Add client-facing status updates (currently owner-only via Discord — raw logs should never reach a client).
- Wrap agent restarts in PM2 for resilience (already standard practice elsewhere in the JARVIS stack — replicate here).
- Localize/timezone-stamp reports per client region (US/UK/AU/CA clients need reports in their own time reference, not SAST).

DAY 2 — QA PASS (1 hour your time)

### 4.5 Staged Autonomy Levels (feeds directly into the ToS — §7.5)

Not every fix should be autonomous. `webdesign-ai` operates on three explicit tiers per client site:

| Level | Scope | Examples | Human involvement |
|-------|-------|----------|----------------------|
| **Level 1 — Auto-fix** | Content, performance, non-destructive layout | Broken links, image compression, lazy-loading, typos, minor CSS/responsive bugs, slow-loading assets | None — agent fixes, logs, notifies after the fact |
| **Level 2 — Propose, don't apply** | Anything touching checkout, forms, pricing, auth | Quote-form logic, booking-flow changes, any code near a payment integration, pricing displayed on the page | Agent drafts the fix and a diff; a single Discord approval button applies it — never auto-applied |
| **Level 3 — Escalate only, no attempt** | Security, data-breach risk, legal copy | Payment gateway integration code, ToS/privacy policy text, anything flagging as a potential compliance issue | Human-only, agent does not attempt a fix, flags and stops |

This tiering is what makes "fully autonomous" a defensible claim instead of a liability — it is disclosed explicitly in the client contract (§7.5), so no client is surprised that an AI touched their site, and none can claim the AI was given free rein over money-handling code.

## 7. LEGAL & OPS — SOUTH AFRICA-BASED, GLOBAL CLIENTS

### 7.1 Entity Structure

**Unchanged recommendation: start as a Sole Proprietor, convert to (Pty) Ltd once monthly revenue consistently exceeds ~R50,000 (~$2,700).** This is an operating-entity decision independent of *where clients are*, and the SA sole-proprietor thresholds remain the relevant ones since Sir is the SA-resident owner regardless of client geography:

- No CIPC registration required; free SARS income tax registration.
- SARS 2026: Turnover Tax threshold doubled to R2.3M; compulsory VAT registration only above R2.3M turnover.
- SA taxes worldwide income — **all Paddle/international payouts must be declared**, not just SA-sourced revenue. Keep records of every payout regardless of the client's country.

Sources: [Tax A Sured — Sole Proprietorship SA 2026](https://www.taxassurance.co.za/understanding-sole-proprietorship-in-south-africa-2026-guide/), [SME South Africa — Small Business Tax 2026](https://smesouthafrica.co.za/a-guide-on-small-business-tax/)

### 7.2 Tax Basics

- Provisional tax submissions twice a year (August + February). Keep 25-30% of revenue in a separate account from day one, converted to ZAR as it's earned to avoid FX-timing surprises at tax time.
- Do not register for VAT early — compulsory only at R2.3M turnover.
- International income is still SA-taxable; Paddle's payout reports plus Wave Accounting invoicing should be enough documentation for SARS eFiling.

### 7.3 Payments — Merchant of Record Recommendation

**Recommendation: Paddle, as the primary and default billing rail for every international subscription client.**

A Merchant of Record (MoR) becomes the legal seller of record — the customer's receipt shows the MoR's name, and the MoR handles all tax calculation, collection, filing, and remittance globally. This matters enormously for a solo SA operator serving the US, UK, and EU: without an MoR, *you* would be personally responsible for tracking and filing US state sales tax, UK VAT, and EU VAT across every jurisdiction a client happens to be in — completely impractical to do correctly as a one-person operation.

| Option | Fee | Tax handling | Verdict |
|--------|-----|----------------|---------|
| **Paddle (recommended)** | 5% + $0.50/transaction | Covers US sales tax (all applicable states), EU VAT (every member state), UK VAT, AU GST, and B2B reverse-charge VAT for EU business clients — broadest jurisdiction coverage of the three. Payouts to South Africa confirmed supported, via wire transfer or Payoneer, processed by the 15th of the month. | Use this as the default. The nominal 5%+ fee is not meaningfully worse than Stripe once Stripe's real-world international surcharges are counted (see below), and Paddle removes 100% of the tax-compliance burden. |
| Lemon Squeezy | Similar MoR model, Stripe-owned since 2023 | Covers US sales tax + EU VAT + a growing list of others, narrower than Paddle | Fine at small scale, but has a practical ARR ceiling around $500K-1M before its billing/contract tooling becomes a constraint — a real risk if this business succeeds. Not worth building around given Paddle covers the same ground with more headroom. |
| Stripe direct (or via Paystack for ZAR) | 2.9%+$0.30 nominal, but effective rate rises to ~4.9-5.2% once international card surcharges (+1.5%), currency conversion (+1%), and Stripe Tax (+0.5%) are included | *You* remain liable for calculating, collecting, and remitting tax in every jurisdiction — Stripe Tax helps calculate but does not file or remit on your behalf | Effective cost is nearly identical to Paddle once real fees are counted, with none of the compliance benefit. ### 7.4 POPIA + GDPR Compliance (Mandatory for EU/UK Clients)

This section did not exist in the prior draft and is required the moment any client site has EU or UK visitors — which will be immediate given the target markets include the UK.

- **Both frameworks use an opt-in consent model.** Neither POPIA nor GDPR permits collecting, tracking, or processing visitor data before affirmative consent — `tracker.js` and `chat-widget.js` must not fire tracking events for a visitor until a consent banner has been accepted. This is a required build-pipeline change, not optional polish: every site template must ship with a consent-gated tracker.
- **POPIA applies extraterritorially.** It applies to any entity — SA-based or not — that processes personal information within South Africa, and SA companies processing EU/UK visitor data are separately subject to GDPR's territorial scope for those visitors. Practically: Sir's agency is a data *processor* for every client site's visitor data, and needs a compliant footing under both regimes simultaneously, not just POPIA because the business is SA-registered.
- **Data subject rights** (access, correction, deletion, notification of collection) must be honored on every client site — build a standard privacy policy template that discloses what `tracker.js`/`chat-widget.js` collect and how long it's retained, and make deletion requests a defined (even if manual, at this scale) process.
- **Client contract needs a Data Processing Addendum (DPA)** for any client whose end-users are EU/UK-based, clarifying that the agency processes visitor data on the client's behalf and under their instruction, with the agency as processor and the client as controller.
- **Practical build items**: a consent-management snippet (even a lightweight one, e.g., a simple accept/reject banner gating `tracker.js` initialization) becomes a standard part of every site launch checklist from day one, not an afterthought added when the first EU-facing complaint arrives.

Sources: [Usercentrics — POPIA vs GDPR](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/popia-vs-gdpr/), [Proofpoint — Ultimate Guide to POPIA](https://www.proofpoint.com/uk/blog/information-protection/ultimate-guide-popia-south-africas-privacy-law), [TechGDPR — Comparison of POPIA and GDPR in Key Areas](https://techgdpr.com/blog/a-comparison-of-popia-and-gdpr-in-key-areas/)

### 7.5 Terms of Service — What the Autonomous Agent Can and Cannot Do

The client contract must explicitly disclose the staged-autonomy model from §4.5, not just "an AI may work on your site" in generic terms:

- **Disclose AI usage explicitly**, including the three-tier autonomy model (auto-fix / propose-only / human-escalation-only) — non-disclosure risks being treated as a material breach if a client reasonably expected human-only work.
- **State plainly**: Level 1 fixes (content, performance, non-destructive layout) are applied automatically and logged; Level 2 changes (checkout, forms, pricing, auth) are only ever proposed and require the agency owner's explicit approval before going live; Level 3 items (security, payment integration code, legal copy) are never auto-attempted.
- **Liability cap**: total liability capped at fees paid over a trailing 3-12 month window; disclaim indirect/consequential damages — standard practice for AI-fulfilled service contracts.
- **Change-request scope**: written inclusion/exclusion list per tier (§3.2); anything outside scope is a separate quote, stated upfront, no verbal exceptions.
- **All autonomous changes are logged and reversible via git** — this is already true of `webdesign-ai`'s `logs.json` and should be stated as a client-facing assurance, not just an internal implementation detail.

### 7.6 Solo-Operator Hours Model — Where a VA Becomes Mandatory

An honest capacity model, not an assumption that AI makes headcount irrelevant forever:

| Client count | New clients/month (10% churn replenishment + modest growth) | Build-related hours (~4 hrs/new client: QA pass + revision review + escalations) | Steady-state care hours (~45 min/client/month: Level-2 approvals, monthly report review, occasional escalation) | Total hours/month | Weekly load |
|---------------|------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| **20 clients** | ~2/mo | ~8 hrs | ~15 hrs | **~23 hrs** | ~5-6 hrs/week |
| **40 clients** | ~4/mo | ~16 hrs | ~30 hrs | **~46 hrs** | ~11-12 hrs/week |
| **60 clients** | ~6/mo | ~24 hrs | ~45 hrs | **~69 hrs** | ~17 hrs/week |

This excludes sales/outreach management time, which scales separately. **At roughly 40-45 clients, Sir's personal review/approval load alone crosses ~10-12 hours/week — this is the point a part-time VA or contractor (10-15 hrs/week) should be brought on**, specifically to handle first-pass QA and Level-2 triage under supervision, freeing Sir for sales, vertical expansion, and genuine exceptions only. Beyond 60 clients without that support, the review-gate discipline in §4.5 (the thing that keeps quality from collapsing under volume — see §9) becomes the first casualty, which is precisely the failure mode this plan is designed to avoid.

Reference: DesignJoy sustains ~35 clients solo with a *human-only* fulfillment model and strict async/queue discipline — our AI-assisted build layer buys some headroom over that benchmark, but the steady-state *care* time (Level-2 approvals specifically) does not shrink just because the build did, which is why the VA threshold above lands lower than DesignJoy's headline client count would suggest.

Source: [Medium — The DesignJoy Blueprint](https://medium.com/@zack_liu/the-designjoy-blueprint-how-1-person-handles-35-clients-at-5-000-month-no-meetings-allowed-6fd59df830fe)

## THE ONE RECOMMENDATION

Run exactly one model: a niche subscription — home service contractors first, dental practices second — sold worldwide from week one, billed through Paddle, built on the v0/Vercel pipeline, and cared for by `webdesign-ai`'s staged-autonomy agent. South Africa is a launchpad for proof, not the market. The currency arbitrage (~88-90% margins on USD revenue against a ZAR cost base) and the self-healing-site claim are the two structural advantages nothing else in this plan can substitute for — protect both: never let scope creep erode the margin, and never let autonomy staging get relaxed under growth pressure. Everything else in this document is tactics; those two things are the moat.
